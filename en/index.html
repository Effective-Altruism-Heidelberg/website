Effective Altruism Heidelberg

A life is priceless. Saving one isn't.

We're a student group in Heidelberg that spends evenings learning which ways of helping people, animals, and the future actually work best — then meets the people doing them. No background required. Just curiosity and an evening to spare.

It starts with one decision

One person really can make a difference

Most of the good anyone ends up doing starts small: one person who decided to look a little closer, show up to a single talk, or bring a friend along. That's genuinely how groups like this one grow. Skip a few coffees this semester, and that's already enough for a dozen malaria nets — real protection for real families, for years. Bring one friend to an event, and next semester there might be two of you doing the same. Small decisions, multiplied, add up faster than you'd think.

In three sentences

What is effective altruism?

Use evidence, not just instinct

Figure out what actually helps, using research and data — not just what feels generous.

Prioritise thoughtfully

Some problems are bigger, more neglected, and more solvable than others. Work on those first.

Then actually act

Through your career, your donations, or your time — ideas only matter once you put them into practice.
